# SOP-EX-07 Trenching and excavation edges

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This procedure describes how to dig trenches and work near excavation edges without causing a collapse or putting people at risk.

### 1.2 Scope
It applies to excavator operators digging trenches or working next to any excavation, including the first trench task on a new site.

## 2. Before digging

### 2.1 Competent person inspection
A competent person must inspect the excavation and the area around it before work starts each shift, after any rain and whenever conditions change (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.651(k)). Do not start digging until the inspection is done.

### 2.2 Buried services
Confirm that buried services such as power, gas and water have been located and marked before you dig. If you uncover an unmarked service, stop digging and report it.

### 2.3 Plan the machine position
Plan where the machine will stand and travel before you dig. Keep the machine back from the trench edge; the SAMPLE site rule is a setback at least equal to the trench depth, unless the competent person approves a different position.

## 3. Working at the edge

### 3.1 Edge setback
Keep the tracks behind the marked setback line while digging and travelling. The weight and vibration of the machine near the edge can cause the trench wall to collapse.

### 3.2 Spoil placement
Place spoil at least 0.6 m (2 ft) back from the trench edge, or further if the competent person requires it (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.651(j)(2)). Never pile spoil where it can slide back into the trench.

### 3.3 Approach speed near the edge
Approach the edge slowly and only in low travel speed. Travel parallel to the trench rather than toward it where possible. Never turn or swing with the bucket loaded toward a person in or near the trench.

### 3.4 Surcharge and vibration
Keep trucks, stockpiles and other heavy loads away from the edge. Heavy loads and vibration close to the edge add pressure on the trench wall.

## 4. Protecting people

### 4.1 People near the trench
No one may be in the trench within reach of the bucket while you are digging. Stop digging if anyone enters the trench or the swing radius, and resume only when they are clear.

### 4.2 Protective systems
Trenches 1.5 m (5 ft) deep or more need a protective system such as sloping, benching, shoring or a trench box, designed or approved by the competent person, unless the trench is in stable rock (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.652(a)).

### 4.3 Getting out of the trench
Trenches 1.2 m (4 ft) deep or more need a safe way out, such as a ladder or ramp, within 7.6 m (25 ft) of travel for anyone in the trench (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.651(c)(2)).

## 5. Warning signs and stop work

### 5.1 Signs of instability
Watch for cracks along the edge, bulging or sliding walls, water seeping into the trench, soil falling from the walls and settling of the ground near the machine. These are signs that the trench wall may collapse.

### 5.2 Stop-work actions
If you see any sign of instability, stop work, move the machine away from the edge if it is safe to do so, warn anyone in or near the trench to get out, and call the supervisor. Do not resume until the competent person has inspected the trench.

### 5.3 Rain and water
Rain and water weaken trench walls. After rain, wait for the competent person to re-inspect the trench before working near the edge again.
