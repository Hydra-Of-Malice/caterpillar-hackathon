# SOP-EX-04 Truck loading with hydraulic excavators

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This procedure describes how to load haul trucks safely and smoothly with a hydraulic excavator. It focuses on the approach to the truck, swing control near the truck and placing the load.

### 1.2 Scope
It applies to excavator operators loading trucks in a marked truck-loading zone, such as zone TL-1 on bench 3. The loading zone is a controlled area: only the excavator and the truck being loaded work inside it.

## 2. Setting up the loading area

### 2.1 Bench and ground
Before loading, check that the bench is level and firm under the tracks and that the dig face is stable. Position the machine so the tracks are square to the dig face and the travel motors are not under the working area.

### 2.2 Truck position
Set up so the truck stands on the operator's side of the machine where you can see the whole truck body. Aim for a short, shallow swing between the dig face and the truck. A smaller swing angle is safer, smoother and uses less fuel.

### 2.3 Signalling the truck
Use the agreed site signal (for example the horn or radio) to tell the truck when to reverse into position and when loading is finished. Do not start loading until the truck has stopped and is in position.

## 3. Approach and swing control

### 3.1 Swing path
Plan the swing path before each cycle. The bucket must travel over the truck body only, never over the truck cab and never over people or other equipment.

### 3.2 Slowing the swing near the truck
Slow the swing as the bucket approaches the truck body. Start to ease off the swing control early, in the last part of the swing, so the upper structure stops without overshooting. A fast swing late in the cycle is the most common cause of bucket contact with the truck and of spilled material.

### 3.3 Bucket height over the truck
Raise the bucket higher than the truck side boards before you swing over them. Keep a clear gap between the bucket and the side boards through the whole swing.

### 3.4 Never over the cab
Never swing the bucket or the load over the truck cab. If the truck is positioned so that the swing would pass over the cab, stop and have the truck repositioned.

### 3.5 When the truck moves
If the truck moves while you are swinging, stop the swing, hold the bucket clear of the truck and wait until the truck has stopped again.

## 4. Placing the load

### 4.1 Dumping the load
Dump the load smoothly, as low over the truck body as the side boards allow, to reduce impact and spillage. Start the first bucket at the front of the body, then load toward the rear.

### 4.2 Load distribution
Spread the load evenly across the truck body and do not overload the truck. Follow the truck's rated payload and the site's load count for the material.

### 4.3 Smooth control
Use smooth, combined movements of the boom, stick, bucket and swing controls. Avoid sudden starts, stops and control reversals. Smooth control reduces shock loads on the machine and makes the bucket position easier to predict near the truck.

## 5. Stopping loading

### 5.1 Stop conditions
Stop loading immediately if a person on foot enters the loading zone or the swing radius, if the truck moves unexpectedly, or if visibility becomes too poor to see the truck body clearly. Resume only when the cause has been cleared.

### 5.2 End of loading
When the truck is loaded, lower the bucket away from the truck and signal the truck to leave. Wait until the truck has pulled clear before starting the next dig cycle toward the truck position.
