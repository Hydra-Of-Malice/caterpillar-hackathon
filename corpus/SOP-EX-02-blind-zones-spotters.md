# SOP-EX-02 Blind zones and spotters

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This procedure explains how to manage blind zones around the machine, how to respond to proximity alerts and how to work with a spotter.

## 2. Blind zones

### 2.1 Know your blind zones
Every machine has blind zones where the operator cannot see a person from the seat, mostly behind the machine and on the side away from the cab. Learn where the blind zones are on the machine you operate.

### 2.2 Mirrors and cameras
Keep mirrors and cameras clean and correctly adjusted. Check them at every pre-start inspection and report any that are damaged or missing.

### 2.3 Walk-around before moving
Walk around the machine before you start it and after any time you leave the cab. Check that no people, vehicles or objects are in the blind zones.

## 3. Proximity alerts

### 3.1 Person-in-zone alert
When the person-in-zone proximity alert sounds, stop all machine motion immediately: swing, travel and attachment.

### 3.2 Resuming after an alert
Resume work only after the person has been accounted for and has moved clear of the machine's working area. If you cannot see the person, keep the machine stopped and contact them or the supervisor by radio.

### 3.3 The system does not replace looking
The proximity system supports your own checks; it does not replace them. If the proximity system is not fitted or shows as degraded, work with extra caution and use a spotter where people may be near the machine.

## 4. Working with a spotter

### 4.1 When a spotter is needed
Use a spotter when people must work near the machine, when you cannot see the area you are moving into, and when working near trench edges, power lines or other hazards.

### 4.2 Agree the signals
Agree the hand signals or radio calls with the spotter before work starts. The spotter must stand where you can see them and outside the swing radius.

### 4.3 Losing sight of the spotter
If you lose sight of the spotter, stop all motion immediately and do not move until you can see the spotter again.

### 4.4 Stop signal
Only the spotter gives movement signals, but anyone may give the stop signal. Always obey a stop signal.

## 5. Before swinging or travelling

### 5.1 Look before you move
Before you swing or travel, pause, look in the direction of movement and check mirrors and cameras.

### 5.2 Horn signals
Sound the horn before starting to travel, using the site horn convention. The SAMPLE site convention is two short blasts before moving forward and three short blasts before reversing.

### 5.3 Reversing
Before reversing, look behind the machine, sound the horn and pause before moving so that anyone nearby has time to react.
